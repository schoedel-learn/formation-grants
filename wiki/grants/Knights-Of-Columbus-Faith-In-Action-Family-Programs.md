**What it funds (and how).** Faith in Action is the Knights of Columbus' program framework — nine
Family programs (Building the Domestic Church Kiosk, Food for Families, Consecration to the Holy
Family, Sacramental Gifts, Quinceañera Support, Family of the Month/Year) plus Faith, Community
and Life categories. There is no grant application a diocesan office can file: money and materials
flow through local and state councils, which plan, budget and run the programs themselves, with
matched-grant models existing only for council-executed efforts (e.g., the Ultrasound Initiative).

**Why we do not qualify — but why this matters anyway.** The eligibility criterion for an
application-based diocesan grant fails: the recipient/actor in every family program is the council,
not the diocesan office. The practical upside is the giving pathway: as grantee dioceses elsewhere
have shown (Owensboro's CMI Fund match was raised partly from 14 local K of C councils), and since
Knights of Columbus Charitable Fund donors — including councils — can recommend grants to parishes
and dioceses, the office's leverage is relationship-based, not application-based.

**What to do next.** Have the Family Life office brief the local council leadership and the state
council family director jointly on marriage/family ministry priorities; invite councils to
designate Faith in Action family-program funds toward the same needs in the diocesan plan. Treat
any K of C support as an outcome of that partnership rather than a grant to chase on a deadline.
