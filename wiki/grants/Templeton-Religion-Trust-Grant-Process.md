**What it funds.** Global projects that enrich the spiritual conversation — "More Spiritual Information," "More 'Benefits of Religion'," and "More Spiritual Growth" (religious innovation leading to improved forms of religious faith and practice). Grants are large and research/innovation-flavored; TRT is one of three Templeton philanthropies and gives multi-million-dollar project grants.

**Why we don't qualify right now.** There is no application route: engagement "begins with an invitation from a program officer to submit a brief prospectus," and TRT "does not accept unsolicited proposals." A (arch)diocesan office seeking a family/marriage/formation program grant cannot initiate; the realistic door is cultivating a TRT program-officer relationship, which is a fundraising development play, not a grant application.

**What to do next.** No apply now. If the office ever wants to pursue Templeton money, identify which TRT program officer covers religious-innovation or US religious-portfolio work and seek one warm introduction; meanwhile monitor the linked Templeton-family watchlist below.

**Templeton-family access map (all checked 2026-10-27).**
- **Templeton Religion Trust (TRT)** — invitation-only; no unsolicited proposals (see above).
- **John Templeton Foundation (JTF)** — has the only open annual application process in the family: an Online Funding Inquiry (OFI). See John-Templeton-Foundation-Online-Funding-Inquiry.md. 2026 OFI deadline passed July 15, 2026; 2027 cycle dates to be announced.
- **Templeton World Charity Foundation (TWCF)** — funds scientific research on flourishing, meaning, and truth; RFPs are announced (subscribe via newsletter — "You're invited to subscribe to our newsletter so you can learn of opportunities to … respond to RFPs"); research-oriented, not formation-program funding.
- **Templeton Ideas** — editorial/content platform, no grantmaking.

## Near-miss excluded (owner quality bar, 2026-10-27 — never own pages)

- Deaconess Foundation (St. Louis): UCC-rooted, children's health systems-change; applicants must be in its eight-county St. Louis footprint — Texas excluded.
- The Dibble Institute: relationship-education curriculum vendor (Love Notes/Mind Matters); not a funder — it helps clients find money to buy its curricula.
- Fetzer Institute: verbatim "we don't currently offer public funding opportunities or accept unsolicited proposals. We also don't fund individual projects, programs, or organizations outside of our ongoing collaborations."
- Mustard Seed Foundation (Falls Church, VA): matching seed grants of ~$500–$30,000 for start-up projects of local churches — below the $50k+ size bar, and its official site (msfdn.org) was unreachable/Cloudflare-blocked, so unverified.
- We Raise Foundation (formerly Wheat Ridge, Lutheran-rooted): Christian nonprofits, but priority granting areas are education, workforce development, and reentry — no formation/marriage/family focus overlap.
- Thrivent Action Teams: ~$250 member-sponsored micro-grants — below the size bar (Lutheran-rooted fraternal; requires a Thrivent-member sponsor).
