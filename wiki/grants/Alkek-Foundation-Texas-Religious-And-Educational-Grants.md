**What it funds.** The Albert and Margaret Alkek Foundation of Houston — established at Albert Alkek's death in 1995 — supports "charitable, religious, scientific (primarily medical), cultural and educational organizations and programs serving the people of the State of Texas." Religious organizations are named in the founding purpose, which is unusual for a large Houston legacy foundation. Unsolicited funding is limited; applicants send a 1–2 page letter of inquiry by US mail describing the organization and project and stating the amount requested, with no deadlines — requests are accepted throughout the year.

**Why we qualify — and why this is a maybe.** The grantor's own page names "religious" organizations statewide but never names Catholic or diocesan entities, so per our qualification filter it stays a maybe rather than eligible. Parish and diocesan faith-formation programs are public charities serving Texas, and the Alkek family's giving history includes Texas Catholic higher education, but that is context, not page-verified eligibility. Also note the stated constraint: "The funding available for unsolicited grants is very limited," and only one request per organization per twelve-month period. Our grants-received records scan (2025–2027) shows no Alkek grants to ArchSA entities.

**What to do next.** Mail a one-to-two-page letter of inquiry to Albert and Margaret Alkek Foundation, 1100 Louisiana, Suite 5250, Houston, TX 77002 — email and fax are explicitly not accepted. State total project funding and the amount requested; no tax returns or detailed budgets. If interested, the Foundation will request documentation. No amounts are published, so calibrate modestly.
