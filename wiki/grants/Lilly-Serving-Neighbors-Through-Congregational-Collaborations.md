**What it funds.** Lilly Endowment's flagship open religion initiative (announced Sept 28, 2026): grants to charitable organizations that broker and strengthen collaborations between Christian congregations and local charitable organizations to address community needs (hunger relief, after-school programs, violence reduction, homelessness, parks, community festivals). Two stages: Stage 1 concept papers due 2027-01-19 with planning grants of up to $50,000 awarded ~March 2027; Stage 2 implementation grants of up to $5 million each over up to four years, announced ~end of November 2027. There is no minimum implementation-grant amount. The initiative's second stated aim — "to deepen the faith of individuals and enhance the vitality of congregations by providing opportunities for Christians to practice their faith through acts of love and care for their neighbors" — makes the parish-faith dimension fundable, not just social services.

**Why we qualify.** A diocesan office or Catholic Charities-style agency is a "regional denominational agency" committed to supporting congregations, which is the named applicant type; the Endowment explicitly excludes individual congregations, so the application must come through the diocesan level, not a single parish. The RFP's tax-status section anticipates denominationally related organizations covered by group exemption rulings, which fits a diocese listed under the USCCB/diocesan group exemption.

**What to do next.** Confirm the diocesan office (or a diocesan social-services arm) will author one concept paper naming 3–5 concrete parish/community collaborations in the diocese; download the Request for Concept Papers from the initiative page and submit via the Serving Neighbors Concept Paper Submission Form by 2027-01-19; email servingneighbors@lei.org with questions. Remaining information-session slot: Nov 10, 3 p.m. (ET) (registration on the initiative page).
